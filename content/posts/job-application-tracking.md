# Job Application Tracking Guide

**WordPress Post Settings:**
- **Category:** Guides & Templates
- **Tags:** job-search, organization, tracking
- **Excerpt:** A simple system to keep track of where you've applied, what stage each application is in, and what to follow up on. Stay organized without expensive tools.

---

When you're applying to multiple positions, it's easy to lose track of where you've applied, what you submitted, and when to follow up. A simple tracking system prevents missed deadlines, duplicate applications, and forgotten opportunities.

You don't need expensive software for this — a spreadsheet or even a notebook works fine.

## Why Track Your Applications?

- **Avoid applying twice** to the same company
- **Know when to follow up** — timing matters in job searches
- **Remember what you submitted** so you can prepare for interviews
- **Spot patterns** in your search (which roles get responses, which don't)
- **Stay motivated** by seeing your progress over time

## The Tracking Template

Use this column structure in a spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel, or LibreOffice Calc):

| Column | What to Record | Example |
|--------|---------------|---------|
| **Company** | Company name | Acme Corp |
| **Position** | Job title | Junior Software Developer |
| **Date Applied** | When you submitted | 2026-10-15 |
| **Source** | Where you found the listing | LinkedIn / Company website / Referral |
| **Resume Version** | Which version of your resume you sent | Resume_v3_Tech.pdf |
| **Cover Letter** | Yes/No + any customization notes | Yes — mentioned their open-source work |
| **Status** | Current stage | Applied / Phone Screen / Interview / Offer / Rejected / No Response |
| **Follow-Up Date** | When to check in if no response | 2026-10-29 (2 weeks after applying) |
| **Notes** | Any relevant details | Referred by Priya. Interviewer was Rahul (Engineering Manager) |
| **Response Date** | When you heard back | 2026-10-22 |

## How to Set This Up

### Option 1: Google Sheets (Recommended)

1. Open [Google Sheets](https://sheets.google.com) and create a new spreadsheet
2. Name it **"Job Application Tracker"**
3. Create the columns listed above in Row 1
4. **Freeze the header row:** View → Freeze → 1 row
5. **Color-code the Status column** for quick scanning:
   - 🟡 Yellow = Applied (waiting)
   - 🔵 Blue = Phone Screen / Interview scheduled
   - 🟢 Green = Offer received
   - 🔴 Red = Rejected
   - ⚪ Gray = No Response (past follow-up date)

**Tip:** Use Google Sheets' **Data Validation** on the Status column to create a dropdown menu:
1. Select the Status column cells
2. Go to Data → Data Validation
3. Set Criteria to "List of items"
4. Enter: `Applied, Phone Screen, Interview, Offer, Rejected, No Response`

### Option 2: Notion or Simple Note App

If you prefer Notion, create a **database** with the same columns. Notion's board view lets you drag applications between status columns like a kanban board.

### Option 3: Paper Notebook

If digital tools aren't your thing, dedicate a notebook to your job search:
- One page per application
- Write the company, role, date, and what you submitted
- Leave space for notes after interviews
- Review weekly to check follow-up dates

## When and How to Follow Up

### The Follow-Up Timeline

| Stage | When to Follow Up | How |
|-------|-------------------|-----|
| After applying (no response) | **2 weeks** after submitting | Brief email to hiring manager or recruiter |
| After a phone screen | **1 week** if no response about next steps | Email your recruiter contact |
| After an interview | **Within 24 hours** — send a thank-you | Then wait 1 week before following up |
| After a final interview | **1 week** after their stated decision timeline | Polite email asking for an update |

### Sample Follow-Up Email (After Applying)

> **Subject:** Following Up — [Position Title] Application
>
> Dear [Hiring Manager / Recruiting Team],
>
> I submitted my application for the [Position Title] role on [date] and wanted to express my continued interest in the opportunity. I believe my background in [relevant skill/experience] aligns well with what you're looking for.
>
> I'd welcome the chance to discuss how I can contribute to your team. Please let me know if there's any additional information I can provide.
>
> Thank you for your time.
>
> Best regards,  
> [Your Name]

### Sample Thank-You Email (After Interview)

> **Subject:** Thank You — [Position Title] Interview
>
> Dear [Interviewer's Name],
>
> Thank you for taking the time to speak with me today about the [Position Title] role. I enjoyed learning more about [specific topic discussed] and I'm excited about the possibility of contributing to [team/project].
>
> Our conversation reinforced my interest in the position, particularly [specific aspect that stood out]. I'm confident that my experience in [relevant skill] would allow me to contribute meaningfully to your team.
>
> Please don't hesitate to reach out if you need any additional information. I look forward to hearing from you.
>
> Best regards,  
> [Your Name]

## Keeping Your Tracker Useful

### Weekly Review (15 minutes)

Set aside 15 minutes each week to:

1. **Update statuses** — Mark any rejections, schedule interviews, note responses
2. **Check follow-up dates** — Send any follow-up emails that are due
3. **Archive old entries** — Move applications older than 6 weeks with no response to "No Response" status
4. **Review patterns** — Are certain types of roles getting more responses? Adjust your approach accordingly.

### Monthly Review (30 minutes)

Once a month:
1. **Count your numbers** — How many applications? How many responses? What's your response rate?
2. **Identify what's working** — Which resume versions, cover letter approaches, or job sources get the best results?
3. **Adjust your strategy** — If you're not getting responses, it may be time to revise your resume, try different keywords, or expand your search criteria.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- ❌ **Not tracking at all** — relying on memory leads to missed follow-ups
- ❌ **Applying to everything** — quality over quantity; 10 tailored applications beat 50 generic ones
- ❌ **Never following up** — many candidates don't follow up at all, so doing it politely makes you stand out
- ❌ **Following up too aggressively** — once per stage is enough; don't email every day
- ❌ **Not customizing each application** — if your tracker shows you sent the same resume everywhere, that's a sign to adjust
